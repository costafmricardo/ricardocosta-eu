About

17 years designing. One year of AI. That changed everything I thought I knew.

The Story

I started in graphic design. Logos, posters, printed things. Seven years of that.

Then digital took over and I moved with it. Websites, apps, interfaces. Three years learning a completely different craft.

Then UX became a discipline and I moved again. Product design for complex systems. Seven more years, the last of them building tools for thousands of industrial users at one of Europe's largest automotive manufacturers.

Then AI arrived. Not gradually. All at once.

Every transition felt like starting over. Every time, the designers who adapted were not the ones who learned the new tool fastest. They were the ones who understood why the old tool mattered and what carried over.

That pattern is what I teach now.

I have been designing AI integrated systems for thousands of users at scale for the past year. I can not show you those projects. But I can show you how someone in that position thinks. The frameworks, the failures, the decisions that matter more than the screens.

I share this through YouTube, a weekly newsletter, and occasionally through direct advisory work with teams navigating AI integration.

What I believe

Screens were never the value.

They were the receipt. The real work happened before the file opened. In the question you asked, the constraint you identified, the direction you chose not to take.

AI did not change what designers do. It revealed what was always true.

If anyone can generate a screen in 30 seconds, what does that make of someone who knows which screen to generate, why, and what to do when it is wrong?

Tools without frameworks are waste disguised as productivity.

I got tired of tutorials that only show perfect demos. When something breaks, and it breaks often, I show the cleanup cost, the time lost, and what I would do differently.

Sponsored or not. Same standard.

If I review a tool, I use it on real projects first. If a sponsor asks me to skip that step, I skip the sponsor.
